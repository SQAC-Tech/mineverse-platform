# MINEVERSE — Volunteer Pitch Scripts

Two pitches for two very different settings. **A** is for the help desk, where you have a
laptop and can show things. **B** is for class-to-class, where you have a mic and a poster
and nothing else.

**One line, if you only remember one line:** *MINEVERSE is a two-day Minecraft-themed coding
event. Teams of two or three start with an empty inventory, mine resources by solving
problems, craft their way up to a diamond pickaxe, and fight the Ender Dragon at the end.*

### Before you speak to anyone

- Eligibility is **first and second year only**, any department. Don't promise a third-year
  they can play.
- **Never** say "you need to know how to code" or "it's for coders". It is built so a
  first-year in their first month can play. Say that instead.
- Don't quote a prize amount or a fee amount you haven't been given.
  *[Ask your OC lead for the current numbers and write them in below before you go out.]*
- Fill these in before your first shift: **Dates —** ____ · **Venue —** ____ ·
  **Registration closes —** ____ · **Prizes —** ____

## A. Help Desk Pitch

*A table, a laptop with the MINEVERSE site open, a poster. Someone slows down as they walk
past. Your job is to get them from "what's this" to registered without them ever sitting
through a rules explanation.*

### Before your shift starts

*[Open the site on the laptop and scroll it to the top of the **HOW IT WORKS** section, not
the hero. That section is the whole pitch on one screen — leave it parked there and reset
it there after every person.]*

### Step 1 — The hook (about 5 seconds)

> "Do you play Minecraft?"

*Wait for the answer. It doesn't matter which way they answer — both go to the same line.*

> **If yes:** "Then you already know how this works. Same game — except you mine by solving
> problems. Come look."
>
> **If no:** "Doesn't matter, it takes ten seconds to explain. Come look."

*Turn the laptop towards them. Don't hand it over yet.*

### Step 2 — Point at the intro text (about 20 seconds)

*Point at the paragraph at the top of **HOW IT WORKS**.*

> "You come in as a team of two or three, and you start with nothing. Every problem your
> team solves mines resources — wood, stone, iron. Resources let you craft better gear, and
> you can trade at the marketplace if you're short of something. You go all the way from a
> wooden pickaxe to a diamond one."

### Step 3 — Scroll slowly through the round cards (about 30 seconds)

*Scroll one card at a time. Let them read the biome names. Don't read the cards out loud —
point at the parts you're naming.*

> "Five rounds, and each one is a different biome. Forest, caves, mountain, the Nether, and
> the End." *(point at the coloured strip of biome names)*
>
> "Every round has a guardian at the end of it" *(point at the **BOSS** slot on a card)*
> "and every round unlocks the next piece of gear." *(point at the **UNLOCKS** slot)*
>
> "Day 1 is three rounds and a qualifier — there's a leaderboard and a PvP round at the end
> of the day. Make the leaderboard and you come back for Day 2. Day 2 is the Nether Portal
> repair, and then the End — that's the Ender Dragon."

### Step 4 — Say the thing they're actually worried about (about 10 seconds)

*Almost everyone is thinking this and most won't ask it. Say it before they have to.*

> "And it's first and second years only, so you're playing against people at the same stage
> as you. You don't need to already be good at this — that's the whole point of it starting
> you at zero."

### Step 5 — The close

*Turn the laptop to face them and put a hand near — not on — the trackpad.*

> "Want to just register now? It takes about five minutes."

*Then walk them through it, don't leave them alone with the form:*

1. Team name.
2. Details for each member — name, email, **college email**, phone, department and
   registration number. *(Two members minimum, three maximum. Add the third with the
   **+** button.)*
3. The team lead gets an **OTP on their college email**. *[Tell them to check the tab now —
   ask them to keep the college mail open before they start, it saves a minute.]*
4. After the OTP, a **payment QR** appears with the amount for their team size. They scan
   and pay.
5. They type in the **UPI transaction ID** and the **name on the UPI account**, and submit.

> "That's you in. We'll send everything else to your college email."

**If the third member isn't with them:** "Two is a full team, register as two." *[Confirm
your current policy on adding a third member later before you promise it.]*

**If they're alone:** "Register with a friend later, or put your name down with me and I'll
put you in touch with other people looking for a team." *[Only say this if your team is
actually keeping a list — check first.]*

### Questions you will get, and short answers

| They ask | You say |
|---|---|
| **Who can join?** | "First and second years, any department, any branch." |
| **How many people in a team?** | "Two or three. Not four — split into two teams." |
| **Do I need to know how to code?** | "No. If you can read simple code and think through a puzzle, you're fine. And you're playing as a team — you split the work." |
| **Is it hard?** | "It's built for first-years. Everyone starts with the same empty inventory, and the game walks you up from there." |
| **I'm a first-year, is it worth it against second-years?** | "Both years play the same event and teams start identical. You're not behind." |
| **How long does it take?** | "Day 1 is a full day. Day 2 is only for the teams that qualify, and it finishes by the afternoon." *[Fill in the actual timings.]* |
| **What do I win?** | "Prizes for the top teams, and certificates." *[Quote only the prize details your OC lead gave you.]* |
| **Is there a fee?** | "Yes, and the exact amount shows up on screen for your team size when you get to the payment step." |
| **What do I bring on the day?** | *[Confirm with your OC lead — laptops per team, ID cards, chargers — and answer with that.]* |
| **Can I register later at home?** | "You can, but registrations close on ____ and seats are limited. It's five minutes, let's just do it here." |

### Don't say

- "It's a coding competition" as your opening line. Lead with the game, not the exam.
- "The rounds get harder." They don't need that picture in their head.
- Anything you're guessing about — fee, prizes, timings. "Let me check and message you" is
  a perfectly good answer, and it gets you their number.

## B. C2C Pitch (class-to-class)

*You walk in with a mic and a poster. No screen. Everything has to be built in their heads,
so keep the pictures concrete — trees, pickaxes, dragons — and keep the sentences short.
Target: 75–90 seconds. Hold the poster up from the start and keep it up.*

### The script

*[Wait until the room is quiet. Don't start over chatter.]*

> "Quick show of hands — how many of you have played Minecraft?"

*[Pause two seconds. Look around, react to whatever the room does — smile, nod. Don't wait
for a big response, keep moving either way.]*

> "Then you know the first night. You've got nothing. You punch a tree, you get wood, you
> make a pickaxe.
>
> That's the event we're running. It's called MINEVERSE. Two days, and your team of two or
> three walks in with an empty inventory.
>
> Every problem you solve mines resources. Resources let you craft — wooden pickaxe, then
> stone, then iron armour. And at the end of every round, there's a guardian standing
> between you and the next biome."

*[Slow down here. This is the part they'll repeat to their friends.]*

> "Day 1 is three rounds — forest, caves, and the mountain — and a leaderboard at the end
> of the day. Make the leaderboard, and you come back for Day 2. Day 2, you repair the
> Nether Portal, and then you go into the End. And the End has a dragon in it.
>
> It's open to first and second years, any department. And you do not need to already be
> good at coding — everybody starts from zero, on purpose. That's the whole design."

*[Lift the poster higher. This is the only ask — make it one clear instruction, not three.]*

> "Scan the QR on this poster and register as a team of two or three. Registrations close
> on ____. Grab two friends on the way out and do it now — it takes five minutes.
>
> Thank you."

*[Leave. Don't linger and don't add a second call to action — one instruction is what they
remember. If people follow you out, take them through the help desk steps.]*

### The 20-second version

*For when the professor gives you a nod and nothing more. Say it standing still, no
hand-raise question, and get out.*

> "Twenty seconds, thank you sir. We're running MINEVERSE on ____ — it's a two-day
> Minecraft-themed coding event. Teams of two or three, first and second years only. You
> mine resources by solving problems, craft your way up to a diamond pickaxe, and fight the
> Ender Dragon on Day 2. You don't need to be a good coder — everyone starts at zero. Scan
> the poster to register. Thank you."

### If someone shouts a question

*Answer in one sentence and go back to the close. Don't get pulled into a Q&A in front of
a class.*

- **"Is it only for CSE?"** — "Any department, first and second year."
- **"Do we need to know coding?"** — "No. Everyone starts at zero and you play as a team."
- **"Is there a fee?"** — "Yes, and the amount is on the registration page." *[Or the number
  your OC lead gave you.]*
- **Anything longer than that** — "Scan the poster, all of it's on the page. I'll be at the
  desk outside if you want to ask more."
